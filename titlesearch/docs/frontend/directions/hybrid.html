What in this brief is wrong

1 · "Once per screen" cannot be literally true on the decision screen, and the brief itself is why. It mandates a red sidebar badge, a red stamp, a red "this page" cell, a red "1 no source" stat, and a solid NO SOURCE tag — five accent occurrences before the Confirm button exists. B could keep its rule because its accent (blue) was a different hue from its status red; adopting A's red as the accent collapses that distinction. I resolved it as: one solid accent action per screen (Take next order · Confirm as read · the override count), errors keep solid red because an uncited value outranks style discipline, and everything else runs at tint-plus-outline strength. If the owner wants the literal rule back, the accent must stop being red.

2 · Fraunces survives the density table only by staying out of it. The h2 and the italic question hold beautifully, but at 13px in 13 rows Fraunces's personality turns to noise and its figures won't sit tabular — so every cell, line and client name in the matrix is Switzer/Geist Mono. That is the honest finding: C's font is a display voice, not a text economy, and the table proves the system works because it never asks Fraunces to do data.

3 · A's warmth costs contrast headroom. ink-3 #8D8471 on card #FAF7EF is ≈4.2:1 — fine at 12px+, marginal for 9.5px tracked caps. Labels under 11px were kept at 600–700 weight to compensate; a production pass should darken ink-3 one step for sub-11px use.

4 · "WAITING 3h 12m" flirts with the no-timers rule. It is an age, not a rate, and the brief asks for it — but it is the closest thing on these screens to a productivity number and should never gain color or sorting.
